Trading glossary

Plain-English definitions for the options-flow, momentum and technical terms used across TickerDesk. Educational, not investment advice.

Options flow

Unusual Options Activity (UOA)
Options trading that stands out from a contract's normal behavior — outsized volume, volume exceeding open interest, or aggressive sweeps. Often read as informed positioning, though on its own it's a filter, not a verdict.
Golden sweep
An aggressive options order large and urgent enough to sweep multiple exchanges and pay the ask — the strongest single conviction footprint in the flow. More on golden sweeps →
Volume / Open Interest (vol/OI)
The ratio of a day's contract volume to its existing open interest. A ratio above 1 means more contracts traded than were previously open — new positioning rather than existing books changing hands.
Open interest (OI) confirmation
Re-reading a flagged contract's open interest the next trading day to see whether open interest rose, barely moved, or fell. A rise is consistent with new positions staying open; it can’t prove a specific trade was held. How OI confirmation works →
Opening buyer
Flow that appears to establish a new position (buying to open) rather than closing an existing one — generally a stronger directional tell.
Premium
The total dollar value of an options trade (price per contract × contracts × 100). Bull vs bear premium tilt summarizes where the money is leaning.

Options structure

Expected move
The market-implied one–standard-deviation price range into an event or expiry, derived from the at-the-money straddle or implied volatility.
Implied vs realized move
What the options are pricing in (implied) versus how much the stock has historically actually moved (realized). A large gap is the earnings-flow edge. Earnings options flow →
Gamma flip
The underlying price where aggregate dealer gamma switches sign — the boundary between a mean-reverting (positive-gamma) and a trend-amplifying (negative-gamma) regime.
OCC-settled
Official open-interest figures settled once per day by the Options Clearing Corporation — the authoritative next-morning OI used for confirmation.

Momentum & setups

VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern)
A base that tightens through progressively smaller pullbacks on declining volume before a breakout, popularized by Mark Minervini. VCP screener →
ADR% (Average Daily Range percent)
A stock's average high-to-low range over recent sessions as a percent of price — how much it typically moves in a day. Higher ADR% means more room and more risk per position.
Relative strength (RS)
How a stock is performing versus the broad market or its peers. High relative strength means it is outperforming — a core momentum filter.
Extension
How far a stock has stretched above a moving average or base. Extended names carry higher pullback (fade) risk.

Model & validation

IC (Information Coefficient)
The rank correlation between a predictive score and realized returns. For daily signals, an absolute IC around 0.05+ is meaningful; near zero means the score has no ranking power — it's a filter, not a ranking.
MFE / MAE
Maximum Favorable Excursion and Maximum Adverse Excursion — the best and worst unrealized moves a signal reached over its window. Together they describe how much heat a trade took and how far it ran.
Expected value (EV)
The average return per signal across all outcomes, winners and losers. A positive EV with a confidence interval that excludes zero is the bar that matters — more than win rate alone.
Profit factor
Gross winnings divided by gross losses. Above 1 means the winners outweigh the losers; right at 1 means no net edge.
Calibration drift
The gap between a model's trained win-rate expectation and its live performance. Persistent negative drift means the tape is paying the signal less than history implied.

TickerDesk labels

Trend grade (A+ … G)
A 20-step grade from the post-close scan: A+ is the strongest uptrend, G the weakest. It describes the chart today, not where the price goes next.
Conviction and opportunity (CONV / OPP)
Conviction counts how many TickerDesk signals agree on a name (trend grade, options flow, momentum lists, themes). Opportunity is that score minus penalties for being stretched or near earnings. Both are ranking aids, not probabilities.
Confidence and calibrated % (conf / cal %)
conf is our own self-assessed score. cal % is how often past picks in the same conviction band actually went the right way the next day; it appears once 30 or more picks have been graded.
Hedge
A sorting bucket on the Desk: a name with a strong trend grade (A or B in an uptrend, F or G in a downtrend) where the options flow is mixed or there is no flow data on the name at all. It doesn’t mean anyone is hedging.
Bounce cohort
Names cut three or more grade notches in one session. In our record this group has bounced slightly more often than not; the Desk shows the measured hit rate and sample size next to it.
OI confirmed
Open interest on a flagged contract rose by the next morning: the next-day open-interest increase is consistent with new positions staying open; it can’t prove this specific trade was held. See open interest confirmation above.
Pullback watch, Breakout watch, Weak trend
One-line labels on the Desk cards, set by simple rules in this order: Weak trend if the name is in a downtrend; Earnings soon if it reports within 5 days; Extended if it is stretched far above its 50-day average or up 8%+ on the day; otherwise by conviction score (how many of our signals agree): Pullback watch for the top tier, Breakout watch for the middle tier, On watch for the rest. The labels don’t check how close price is to a pullback level or a breakout point. They are research labels, not instructions to buy or sell.
Risk-on, Risk-off, Mixed tape
How many stocks are rising versus falling across our universe (breadth). Risk-off means breadth is 42% or lower; the Desk then shows how long setups have fared in our overall outcome record (all market conditions, not only risk-off days).

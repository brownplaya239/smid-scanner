What a research report looks like

Every TickerDesk report is an 8–12 page PDF generated on demand by Claude, structured the same way. Below is an annotated walkthrough of the sections — the layout is real; the example content is illustrative.

This is a format sample, not live analysis. Your report is generated fresh at request time against current market data, filings and flow for the ticker you choose. It is educational research, not investment advice — nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.

Executive takeaway

A two-to-three sentence synthesis up top: the setup in plain language, the single most important thing to watch, and where the thesis breaks. No hedging essay — the decision-relevant read first.

Illustrative: "Momentum leader consolidating above the 50-day after an earnings gap; bulls want the gap held, bears point to a rich multiple into a decelerating growth print."

Bull & bear case

Bull case

The strongest arguments for upside — catalyst path, relative strength, positioning and valuation support — each stated as a specific, checkable point.

Bear case

The strongest arguments against — the risks that would invalidate the setup, competitive or macro headwinds, and what the skeptics see.

A report that only argues one side isn't research. Both cases are built to be genuinely strong.

Recent options flow

The notable unusual options activity in the name: sweeps, volume vs open interest, opening-buyer premium tilt, and whether the flow confirmed on next-day open interest — pulled from the same engine as the live flow scanner.

Key levels

Prior-day high / resistance—
50-day moving average—
Volume point of control—
Expected-move band (options-implied)—

Real reports fill these with the ticker's actual levels and a short read on which matter into the current setup.

Filings & catalysts

A read on recent SEC filings (via EDGAR) and the upcoming catalyst calendar — earnings date, expected move into the print, and any scheduled events that could move the stock.

Bottom line

A closing paragraph that ties the flow, levels, and fundamentals into one clear statement of where the edge is and what would change the view — the same discipline as the rest of the desk: say what's actionable, and where it isn't.

What a research report looks like

Every TickerDesk report is a 6-page equity research PDF plus an evidence appendix, built on demand from market data and SEC filings. No AI model writes it. Below is a page-by-page walkthrough — the layout is real; the example content is illustrative.

This is a format sample, not live analysis. Your report is built fresh at request time from current market data and filings for the ticker you choose. It is educational research, not investment advice — nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.

Equity Research · [Your Ticker]

Built on demand · market data about 15 minutes delayed · every figure sourced in the appendix

Page 1 · Investment summary

A fundamental rating and a separate tactical (price-trend) rating, the Street consensus where it is available, the thesis in a few sentences, and the key risks. A figure the sources can't support is shown as “unavailable” with the reason, never guessed.

Page 2 · Business model and competitive position

Who the customer is, how the company makes money and where it competes, taken from cited filings rather than a generic profile.

Page 3 · Latest quarter and estimate context

The most recent reported quarter from the 10-Q or 10-K, and how it compares with estimates where those are available.

Page 4 · Valuation

Valuation on real inputs only — for example trailing P/E and enterprise value — each with the formula it came from.

Page 5 · Price and technicals

Chart with 20/50/200-day averages and volume—
Relative strength vs SPY—
Support and resistance levels—

Real reports fill these with the ticker's actual chart and levels, plus a short technical read.

Page 6 · Catalysts, risks and monitoring

Upcoming catalysts, the risks ranked by the filing evidence, the key debate on the stock, and the price levels that would confirm or invalidate the tactical view.

Appendix · Evidence and methodology

Everything the six pages cite but don't print: the source inventory, the estimates ledger, the valuation method and derived-figure formulas, Form 4 insider transactions, institutional filings, options data, evidence that was rejected and why, and a sample of social posts. Any number in the report can be traced to where it came from.

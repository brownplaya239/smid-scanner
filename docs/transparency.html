Does options flow actually work?

Numbers as of November 19, 2026 · rebuilt from the live data every weeknight · the live Performance dashboard updates more often · every figure is self-graded against real closing prices, never estimated

Most options-flow products show you the winners. We log every alert our scanner publishes (33,608 so far), wait for real market closes, and grade all of them. Wins, losses, and the cohorts that turned out to be noise. One alert is a contract's first flag of the day; repeat scans of the same contract are folded in. Results measure the underlying stock against SPY, in the flow's direction (a bought put counts as a bet on the stock falling), not option P&L.

Does the flow itself add anything?

Part of any result is simply how the flagged stocks drifted that week. Our research engine splits each book into that drift and the part the flow's direction adds on top:

  • Puts bought (7,070 alerts): 54% beat SPY in the flow's direction, +0.39% average vs SPY. After removing that day's drift of all flagged stocks, the flow itself adds 0.00% (95% range −0.40% to +0.40%), not distinguishable from zero.
  • Calls bought (13,705 alerts): 44% beat SPY in the flow's direction, −0.63% average vs SPY. After removing that day's drift of all flagged stocks, the flow itself adds −0.11% (95% range −0.42% to +0.19%), not distinguishable from zero.
  • Premium sold (7,954 alerts): 50% beat SPY in the flow's direction, −0.26% average vs SPY. After removing that day's drift of all flagged stocks, the flow itself adds −0.27% (95% range −0.59% to +0.05%), not distinguishable from zero.

So when put buyers look right, it is mostly because the stocks they were flagged on drifted down anyway, not because the puts predicted it.

What we've found doesn't work

  • Our score barely ranks. The rank correlation (IC) between our trade score and the result is 0.005 across 20,775 alerts; we treat 0.05 or more as meaningful. The top tenth of scores beat the bottom tenth by 0.06 points. We use the score as an admission filter, not a ranking.
  • Holding longer makes it worse. Average excess vs SPY: +5d −0.28% (not distinguishable from zero); +10d −0.62% (measurably negative); +20d −1.12% (measurably negative).
  • Most ideas fail. Our hypothesis lab has tested 194 feature combinations against later, out-of-sample data. 3 survived and 191 are in the graveyard. After correcting for testing so many, 0 still pass. Survivors are candidates for review, not proven signals. (Lab last run November 18, 2026.)
  • Momentum lists fade. Names on the QM Monthly and Stockbee Weekly lists won 42% of the time with a −2.03% average over the next 5 sessions (1,538 graded).

How the grading works

  • Every published alert is logged the moment it appears: no retroactive additions, no deletions.
  • Returns are graded against real closing prices at +1/+3/+5/+10/+20 days, benchmarked against SPY.
  • Next-day open-interest checks tell us whether open interest rose, which is consistent with new positions staying open (it can't prove a specific trade was held).
  • Cohort statistics wait for a minimum sample and ship with 95% ranges. Below the minimum they say "accruing", never a made-up number.
  • A nightly calibration check compares trained expectations with the live tape and publishes the drift, currently −7 to −4 points depending on score band.
What this isn't: a track record of trades, a promise of returns, or financial advice. It's the measured behavior of a signal engine, published so you can judge it the way we do. Data is 15-minute delayed. Transaction costs are not modeled and matter at these margins.
